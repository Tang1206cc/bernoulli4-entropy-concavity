AN ANALYTIC CURVATURE PROOF FOR THE 23 FOUR-WEIGHT TEMPLATES
======================================================================

Status and scope
----------------
This is a complete analytic proof of strict concavity for each of the 23
templates, conditional only on the separate exact classification showing
that these templates cover all four strictly positive real weights.
It needs no interval arithmetic, sampled derivatives, or numerical logs.
With natural logarithms it gives H''(p)<=-16+6 log(6). Equality holds
exactly when all weights are equal and p=1/2.

Notation
--------
Let p in (0,1), q=1-p, r=pq, and h(p)=-p log p-q log q.
For four independent Bernoulli(p) bits, their joint entropy is 4h(p).
For each fiber of equal weighted subset sums, merge the corresponding
atoms.  If their probabilities are z_1,...,z_j, the entropy loss is

  (z_1+...+z_j) log(z_1+...+z_j) - sum_i z_i log z_i.

The entropy of the weighted sum is 4h minus the sum of these losses.
Complementary subsets yield complementary fibers and p<->q symmetry.

Every merged fiber in the 23 templates is of one of the following kinds:

1. a singleton atoms, and its complementary fiber of a tripleton atoms:

   a*r*(1-2r)*log a.

2. b doubleton atoms in a single fiber:

   b*r^2*log b.

3. a singleton atoms plus b doubleton atoms, together with the
   complementary fiber of b doubleton atoms plus a tripleton atoms:

   F_ab(p)=p*q^2*[(a*q+b*p)log(a*q+b*p)-a*q log q-b*p log p]
           +p^2*q*[(a*p+b*q)log(a*p+b*q)-a*p log p-b*q log q].

   Only (a,b)=(1,1),(1,2),(2,1),(1,3) occur.

4. One singleton and its complementary tripleton in one central fiber:

   T(p)=r*[(p^2+q^2)log(p^2+q^2)-2p^2 log p-2q^2 log q].

Thus every template has the exact identity

  H(p)=4h(p)-A*r*(1-2r)-B*r^2
               -f11*F11-f12*F12-f21*F21-f13*F13-t*T,             (1)

where A,B>=0 and f11,f12,f21,f13,t are nonnegative integers.
The table at the end lists all coefficients.  The accompanying script
reconstructs them from the sixteen subsets, using exact integer counts.

LEMMA 1: THE FOUR MIXED-FIBER CURVATURE BOUNDS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Throughout (0,1),

  F11''(p) >= -1-2 log 2,
  F12''(p) >= -4/3-4 log 3,
  F21''(p) >= -4/3-2 log 3,
  F13''(p) >= -3/2-12 log 2.                                    (2)

All four attain equality at p=1/2.

Proof. Put u=2p-1, so |u|<1. Each F_ab is even and analytic in u.
Write F_ab=sum_{n>=0} c_n u^(2n).  The first three coefficients are:

          c_0                 c_1                     c_2
 F11      log2/4             -log2/4-1/8               5/48
 F12      3log3/8            -log3/2-1/6               19/162+log3/8
 F21      3log3/8            -log3/4-1/6               25/162-log3/8
 F13      log2               -3log2/2-3/16             15/128+log2/2

Every c_2 is positive. For F21 this follows, for instance, from
log3<11/10, since 25/162-11/80>0.

For transparency, a common formula producing all later coefficients is
as follows. Let C=(a+b)/2 and alpha=(b-a)/(a+b). If

  F_ab/r = sum_{n>=0} g_n u^(2n),

then, for n>=2,

  g_n = C/[2n(n-1)(2n-1)] *
        {alpha^(2n-1)[n+(n-1)alpha]-[(n-1)+n alpha]}.             (3)

Consequently c_n=(g_n-g_(n-1))/4 for n>=3. Equation (3) follows
immediately by expanding log(1+u), log(1-u), log(1+alpha*u), and
log(1-alpha*u) in the displayed definition of F_ab.

Here are fully explicit forms, so no sign assertion is hidden in (3).
Set D_n=n(n-1)(n-2)(2n-1)(2n-3)>0, n>=3.

F11:
  c_n=1/8 * {1/[(n-1)(2n-3)]-1/[n(2n-1)]}>0.

F12:
  c_n={9^n(8n^2-19n+9)-(96n^3-144n^2+21n+9)}/[8*9^n D_n]>0.

Indeed 8n^2-19n+9>=24. Also
  (96n^3-144n^2+21n+9)/9^n <=96n^3/9^n <=32/9<24.
The last inequality follows because n^3/9^n decreases for n>=3:
its next-term ratio is <=(4/3)^3/9<1, and its value at n=3 is 1/27.

F21:
  c_n={9^n(4n^2-14n+9)+(48n^3-36n^2+6n-9)}/[8*9^n D_n]>0.

Both terms in the numerator are positive for n>=3: the first
quadratic is increasing there and equals 3 at n=3; the cubic is
12n^2(4n-3)+6n-9>0.

F13:
  c_n=3*{4^n(4n^2-9n+4)-(12n^3-14n^2-6n+4)}/[8*4^n D_n]>0.

Here 4n^2-9n+4>=13, whereas the subtracted term divided by 4^n is
at most 12n^3/4^n<=81/16<13. Again n^3/4^n decreases for n>=3.

Termwise differentiation is valid on compact subintervals of |u|<1.
Since every c_n with n>=2 is positive,

  F_ab''(p)=8c_1+4*sum_{n>=2}(2n)(2n-1)c_n u^(2n-2) >=8c_1.

Reading off 8c_1 from the table gives (2).                         QED

Optional short proof for F11 alone:
F11=r*h and F11''=log r+3(2p-1)log(p/q)-1. Writing u=2p-1 gives
log(4r)+u log(p/q)=2[log2-h(p)]>=0 and u log(p/q)>=0, yielding
F11''>=-1-2log2 without series.

LEMMA 2: THE SINGLETON-TRIPLETON CURVATURE BOUND
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

  T''(p)>=-2.                                                   (4)

Proof. By symmetry put u=|2p-1| in [0,1). Define

  O(u)=(1+u)log(1+u)-(1-u)log(1-u).

O''(u)=-2u/(1-u^2)<=0, O(0)=0, O(1)=2log2, with endpoint values
understood by continuity. Hence concavity gives O(u)>=2u log2.
Since 2(1+u^2)<=4,

  O(u)>=u log[2(1+u^2)].

Direct differentiation of T gives the identity

  T''(p)=-2(1-u^2)/(1+u^2)
          +6u{O(u)-u log[2(1+u^2)]}.

The second term is nonnegative and the first is at least -2.     QED

THE UNIFORM CURVATURE CONCLUSION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Use L2=log(2), L3=log(3), and set

  K=B+f11(1+2L2)+f12(4/3+4L3)+f21(4/3+2L3)+f13(3/2+12L2)+2t,
  D=6A-3B-f13(45/8+24L2).

Since h''=-1/r, [r(1-2r)]''=-6(1-4r), and (r^2)''=2-12r,
the midpoint values in the loss bounds give H''(1/2)=-16+K.
Subtracting that value from H''(p), the joint entropy contributes
-4(1-4r)/r, and the A and B terms contribute (6A-3B)(1-4r).
All other terms give nonpositive differences. For F13, keeping the
quartic coefficient 15/128+L2/2 gives the stronger estimate

  F13''(p)-F13''(1/2) >= (45/8+24L2)(1-4r).

Consequently

  H''(p)-H''(1/2) <= (1-4r)(D-4/r).

The first atanh-series terms give L2>2/3 and L3>1. The positive
exponential series through degree five gives L2<7/10 and L3<11/10.
At equal weights D=30L2-18L3<3, and for (1,1,1,2) it is
18L3-24L2-45/8<-73/40. For (1,1,1,3), (1,1,1,4), and
(1,1,3,3), D=0. All other rows have D<=6A<=12L2<42/5.
Thus D<16<=4/r, giving a strict midpoint maximum for every type.

The equal-weight row has K=6 log(6)>10. Every other row has
K<=99/10, by the rational upper bounds in the last column.
Therefore H''(p)<=-16+6 log(6), with equality exactly when
the weights are all equal and p=1/2. The bound is negative
because 6 log(6)<54/5.

Entropy is continuous at p=0 and p=1. Complement symmetry gives
H(p)=H(1-p), so strict concavity makes p=1/2 the unique maximum
on [0,1]. For kappa=16-6 log(6), the function
H(p)+(kappa/2)p^2 is concave and has no affine segment of positive
length, since its second derivative vanishes at most at p=1/2.
This gives the sharp strong-concavity inequality

  H(lambda*p_0+(1-lambda)*p_1)
    >=lambda*H(p_0)+(1-lambda)*H(p_1)
       +(kappa/2)lambda(1-lambda)(p_0-p_1)^2.

Equality here holds exactly for p_0=p_1 or lambda=0 or 1.

What remains external to this proof
----------------------------------
The completeness of the 23-template classification must be cited from
the independently checked classification artifact. Integer scanning up
to a cutoff would not be sufficient; the exact hyperplane-intersection
classification supplies the required statement for all real weights.

No conclusion about five or more summands is established here. No
conclusion about joint concavity in four different Bernoulli parameters
is claimed. The latter, stronger-looking property already fails with
three summands and weights (1,1,2), as recorded in the original MO thread.
