EXACT CLASSIFICATION OF FOUR POSITIVE WEIGHTED BERNOULLI ENTROPY TEMPLATES
======================================================================

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
Let X_1,...,X_4 be independent Bernoulli(p) variables with a common parameter
p, and let w=(w_1,...,w_4) have arbitrary strictly positive REAL entries.
For every subset S of {1,2,3,4}, put s_w(S)=sum_{i in S} w_i. Partition the
16 subsets by equality of s_w(S). For a class C define its cardinality-count
vector

    N(C)=(N_0(C),...,N_4(C)),  N_k(C)=#{S in C: |S|=k}.

The entropy template is the MULTISET of these vectors, one vector for each
distinct attainable sum. Repeated equal vectors must not be discarded.
The corresponding probability polynomials and entropy are

    F_C(p)=sum_{k=0}^4 N_k(C) p^k (1-p)^(4-k),
    H_w(p)=-sum_C F_C(p) log F_C(p).

There are exactly 23 templates for arbitrary positive real w. In fact there
are exactly 23 subset-equality types up to permutation of the four variables,
and these 23 types produce different multisets N(C). This last statement is
about combinatorial templates, not an assertion that no two resulting entropy
functions could satisfy an additional analytic identity.

Every template has a positive INTEGER representative with maximum entry at
most 7. The bounded integer scan is used only to choose short representatives;
the completeness proof does not assume bounded, rational, or integer weights.

The classification is an exact computer-assisted finite proof using rational
row reduction and exhaustive vertex enumeration. It is not a Lean/Coq kernel
formalization. All operations needed for the finite certificate are specified
below and implemented with the Python standard library, Fraction arithmetic,
and arbitrary-precision integers.

Completeness proof
------------------

1. There are only 25 relevant hyperplanes.

If two different subsets S,T have the same sum, cancel their intersection.
The resulting equality has the form h.w=0 where h has entries in {-1,0,1},
contains both signs, and h and -h describe the same hyperplane. Positivity of
w rules out a nonzero normal whose nonzero entries all have the same sign.
Conversely every such mixed-sign vector describes equality of two disjoint
subsets. There are exactly

    (3^4 - 2*2^4 + 1)/2 = 25

hyperplanes. They consist of six 1=1 relations, twelve 1=2 relations, four
1=3 relations, and three 2=2 relations, where the numerals count the terms
on the two disjoint sides. The implementation fixes the sign by requiring
the first nonzero coordinate of h to be +1.

2. Every real-weight equality pattern appears among 522 rational row spaces.

For an actual positive real w, let R_w be the span over the rationals (or the
reals, with the same rank) of all normals h from the 25-element list satisfying
h.w=0. Since w is nonzero, rank(R_w)<=3. A basis therefore consists of at most
three normals from the list. It suffices to row-reduce every subset of zero,
one, two, or three normals and deduplicate the canonical RREF bases. The
numbers of combinations examined are 1,25,300,2300. The distinct row spaces
have counts

    rank       0    1    2    3       total
    count      1   25  181  315       522.

No numerical test of a real weight is needed in this enumeration.

3. Exact feasibility with strictly positive weights is decidable by vertices.

For a row space R with basis A and rank r, put L=ker(A) and d=4-r. Consider

    P={x: A x=0, sum_i x_i=1, x_i>=0 for i=1,...,4}.

This is a compact polytope (possibly empty). Every vertex can be found by
adding d-1 independent active coordinate constraints x_j=0 to A x=0 and
sum_i x_i=1, then solving the resulting square system. Enumerating every
(d-1)-element coordinate subset is complete even when P has smaller actual
dimension, since any vertex has enough independent active constraints in the
ambient affine space. Singular systems are skipped. All solutions and sign
tests are rational and exact.

The row space admits a strictly positive vector if and only if, for each
coordinate i, at least one vertex of P has positive ith coordinate. Necessity
follows because P is the convex hull of its vertices. For sufficiency, sum all
vertices: the sum has all coordinates positive and belongs to L. Normalizing
the sum is optional. Vertices are stored as primitive nonnegative integer
rays, which have the same coordinate supports and span the same cone.

The feasibility results are

    rank              0    1    2    3       total
    strictly positive 1   25  115   75       216
    excluded          0    0   66  240       306.

For every excluded row space, the JSON records every vertex ray and at least
one coordinate that vanishes on them all. This is a transparent obstruction
to a strictly positive solution.

4. Each feasible row space really occurs with exactly its forced equalities.

For a feasible R let v_0,...,v_{m-1} be its primitive nonnegative vertex rays.
Their span is L: the cone L intersect the nonnegative orthant has nonempty
relative interior because it contains a strictly positive vector. Define

    w(t)=sum_{j=0}^{m-1} t^j v_j,   t a positive integer.

Every coordinate of w(t) is positive. A listed hyperplane h contains L exactly
when h belongs to R. If h is not in R, at least one h.v_j is nonzero, so h.w(t)
is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most m-1. It vanishes at no more than m-1
real numbers. Consequently, among

    1,..., (# hyperplanes not containing L)*(m-1)+1

there is a positive integer t avoiding ALL hyperplanes not containing L.
The script finds one by exact evaluation and records it. Thus every feasible
row space yields a genuine positive integer weight vector with exactly the
claimed collision pattern. In particular, no artificial pattern has been
introduced merely by taking an intersection of hyperplanes.

For the actual R_w in part 2, the same pattern assertion is immediate: if a
listed normal vanishes on w then it was included in the generating set of
R_w, and if it belongs to R_w then it vanishes on w. Hence arbitrary positive
REAL weights and the certified integer witnesses have the same catalogue.

5. Recover the subset partition and quotient by variable relabeling.

For a certified row space R, two subsets S,T are in the same class exactly
when their incidence-vector difference belongs to R. Check this by reducing
the difference against the RREF basis. Independently, evaluate all 16 exact
integer subset sums at its witness and group equal sums. These two partitions
agree for every one of the 216 positive flats.

For each class, count subsets of cardinalities 0,1,2,3,4. Sort the resulting
vectors, preserving multiplicity. This produces exactly 23 different tuples.
Every template satisfies the invariant

    sum_C N(C)=(1,4,6,4,1).

Permute the four coordinates in all 24 ways, row-reduce again, and group row
spaces into S_4 orbits. There are 1,4,10,8 orbits in ranks 0,1,2,3 respectively,
hence 23 in total. Each orbit has precisely one template, and different orbits
have different templates. The orbit sizes are given in the table above and
sum to 216. This independently checks the relabeling symmetry of the output.

6. The finite catalogue reduces entropy calculations exactly.

For any p in [0,1], the probability of the outcome associated to C is F_C(p)
because each individual subset S occurs with probability
p^|S| (1-p)^(4-|S|). Once the multiset of N(C) is known, the numerical support
locations do not enter Shannon entropy. Therefore proving a statement about
H_w(p) for all 23 listed templates proves it for every quadruple of strictly
positive real weights. This classification does not itself prove concavity.

The endpoints p=0,1 can be handled by continuity with the convention 0 log 0=0.
The combinatorial partition is fixed by the weights and is independent of p.
Weight-zero limits are outside the stated domain; repeated positive weights
and every lower-dimensional collision stratum are included explicitly.

Execution and certificate structure
-----------------------------------
The three files in this component are:

    proof/classification.py        generator and finite-certificate checks
    proof/classification.json      exact enumeration, witnesses, and exclusions
    notes/classification_notes.txt this derivation and table

Run from the repository root:

    python proof/classification.py
    python proof/classification.py --verify proof/classification.json

The first command reconstructs the classification from the 25 normals, writes
the JSON, reads its serialized form, and verifies the result. The second
reconstructs the finite row-space universe and validates a supplied JSON.
The verification checks complete row-space coverage, no duplicates, all
vertex lists, every feasibility obstruction, every exact-stratum positive
witness, all subset partitions and count-vector templates, S_4 orbits, and
the short representative for each template.

The optional --example-bound option changes only the bounded scan for short
representatives. It cannot remove or add templates to the complete catalogue.
The default 24 examines C(27,4)=17,550 nondecreasing integer quadruples. All
23 templates already have a representative whose largest weight is at most 7.

Certificate fields most useful for the analytic stage:

    templates[].id
    templates[].count_vectors
    templates[].vector_multiplicities
    templates[].small_integer_example
    templates[].representative_equality_basis

The complete flat-level data are under positive_flats and excluded_flats.
Rational row-space entries are serialized as exact strings such as "-1/3".
Subset masks use bit i for variable i+1, and bit_count(mask) is |S|.

Verification performed
----------------------
The full generator and certificate verifier completed successfully with:
25 hyperplanes; 522 row spaces; 216 positive flats; 306 excluded flats;
23 permutation orbits; 23 templates; no missing representative in the
auxiliary scan. Small-integer witnesses are independently checked by direct
integer subset-sum grouping, so accidental extra collisions are detected.
